UCC Rocketry and Space Exploration Society's launch tests of Ireland's first SRAD Flight Computer were ran under the Theseus launch program, featuring 3 distinct rockets, each with a different goal. Orthrus marked the conclusion of our research before Mach '26.

# Mission Goals

## Theseus 1

Theseus 1's main goal was to prove that the sensors and AV Bay design were sufficient for a rocket launch

## Theseus 2/3

Theseus 2/3's main goal was to prove that our radio telemetry systems were sufficient for a rocket launch, and that it could withstand a second flight.

## Orthrus

Orthrus' main goal was to test two-stage capability of our rockets and ensure the flight computer could function and work with other two-stage computers. It also marked the third and final test flight of this computer.

# Theseus 1

## Avionics Bay

The avionics bay for Theseus 1 feautured a Telemini COTS computer for apogee detection and nose cone separation to deploy parachutes. Grasshopper flew as purely a telemetry-logging computer with no control over the rockets systems.

The design of the avionics bay featured a simple flat plastic sled with mounting holes drilled pre-launch. 

## Results

The rocket lanched succesfully, logging telemetry to onboard systems. This included atitude, temperature, UV and more.

Due to an unidentified issue, we failed to receive radio telemetry from the rocket once it left the launch rail.

## Learnings

The radio blackout was caused by suspected movement of the antenna in the avionics bay (the antenna was taped in pre-flight) causing a short on the board by the radio module. From this flight onwards, the rocket's avionics bay design was led by myself and [John](https://www.linkedin.com/in/john-magner1), instead of the structural team.

The arming keys were not positioned correctly due to an oversight from the structural team. This also contributed to the avionics team taking over the bay design.

Onboard memory was partially corrupted due to insufficient allocation of storage to the data-saving.

# Theseus 2

## Avionics Bay

The avionics bay for Theseus 1 feautured a Telemini COTS computer for apogee detection and nose cone separation to deploy parachutes. Grasshopper flew as purely a telemetry-logging computer with no control over the rockets systems.

It featured a redesigned mounting sled with dedicated slots for batteries and antennas, building on the knowledge from the previous launch. There were also dedicated standoffs for mounting flight computers. It was 3D printed in PLA.

![Theseus 2 AV Bay](./assets/theseus2.webp)

## Results

A faulty motor led to damage to the avionics bay, caused by the motor flying into it. This meant we unfortunately lost the Telemini. Thanks to the new AV Bay design, the components are positioned at the top of the sled, meaning the SRAD was not lost and was shielded from any damage.

The rest of the rocket remained unharmed, meaning all parts were flyable given a replacement COTS computer and motor was found.

![Theseus 2 AV Bay](./assets/theseus2dmg.webp)

## Learnings

Use redundancy for flight computers.

Reposition flight computers to have at least one COTS fully shielded from outside elements.

# Theseus 3

## Avionics Bay

Theseus 3 was flown the same day with the same components as it's predecessor, forgoing the flight computer and sled. A EasyMega was used with the same simple flat plastic sled from Theseus 1.

## Results

The rocket lanched succesfully, logging telemetry to both onboard and ground systems. The onboard telemetry was logged at 4Hz and the ground station received at 1Hz.

## Learnings

A new flow is needed for receiving and logging data. Normal methods of manual CSV parsing was slow and tedious. This led to the development of proprietary software, codenamed Project Zenith.

# Orthrus

## Avionics Team

As the only present avionics member for this launch, I took charge of:

- COTS Two-stage programming.
- Avionics bay wiring.
- Avionics bay assembly.
- Telemetry parsing and recovery.

It was only my second launch, and I was chosen to press the launch button.

![Orthrus](./assets/orthrus2.webp)

## Avionics Bay

Orthrus featured two flight computers. The Telemega and Grasphopper. The Telemega is capable of orchestrating two stage launches with 4 extra configurable pyro channels. 

It featured an updated Avionics bay, with a dual stack design of both computers, with the Telemega on the bottom. This gave the Telemega extra protection while fitting both into the confined space.

This launch was the first time the new software package for telemetry data was tested. The unified groundstation had also begun development, with a target of late 2026 for first test.

## Results

The launch went flawlessly, and all parts were recovered undamaged. This marked the second fully operational flight of Grasshopper.

The flight achieved stage seperation and hot stage.

It marked the FIRST two-stage SRAD rocket ever launched on the island of Ireland.

# Conclusion

All 3 launches proved very helpful in the development of the SRAD Flight computer Grasshopper, and proved it was ready for competition use.
